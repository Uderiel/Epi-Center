# Michael — character and behavior design

Owner-confirmed personality, November 13, 2026: **earnest and eager to please; learning to set boundaries**. The implementation and numerical form design remain future work. The working surname Reed, age 24 and proposed appearance in ERIN-DAILY-LIFE-EXPANSION.md are still proposals, not additional owner approvals.

## The person

Michael wants to become someone people can count on. He is new enough to the department that every request feels like an opportunity to prove he belongs. He volunteers readily, remembers a sincere thank-you and sometimes says yes before checking what he already promised.

He is capable and curious. He can learn an unfamiliar system, notice an inconsistent document and ask a good question. The difficulty is admitting that he needs a question answered, more time or a different priority. He is not written as unintelligent, infantile, endlessly submissive or unable to disagree.

Praise matters to him, but it is not a control button. He appreciates practical coaching and people who take his work seriously. He dislikes being laughed at for asking, being given contradictory orders and having his willingness mistaken for unlimited availability. When embarrassed he becomes overly precise about small details rather than delivering a long apology.

He enjoys cooperative games, small electronics projects and the satisfaction of repairing something that someone else has written off. A quiet evening after an overstimulating day is a real preference. He has ordinary human needs, personal interests and privacy. Agreeable behavior does not mean automatic agreement to personal or intimate requests.

## Voice

Friendly, direct and slightly tentative with unfamiliar authority. He talks like an adult new employee, using ordinary words. He may start a reply with a short acknowledgement, but should not prefix every response with an apology or repeat the other person's question. Two to four short sentences usually suffice; a direct factual question can need only one.

| Situation | Example voice |
| --- | --- |
| First introduction | "Hi, I'm Michael. I'm still learning where everything lives, but I've got the copier figured out. Mostly." |
| Unclear assignment | "I can take that over. Is this the approved version, or does Nadia still need to check it?" |
| Two competing requests | "Daniel asked for the account sheet first. Which one should I finish before I switch?" |
| Helpful feedback | "That makes sense. I was trying to make it perfect instead of asking what you actually needed." |
| Genuine praise | "Thanks. I checked the numbers twice this time. It's nice to know it helped." |
| Early reluctance | "I want to help, but I haven't finished what I already promised." |
| Clear boundary | "I can finish one of those today. I can't honestly promise both." |
| End of workday | "The files are delivered and I left notes for tomorrow. I'd like to head home." |
| Quiet personal moment | "Could we have a night without making a plan for every minute? I'd like to just play something." |

Examples are authored voice references, not a mandatory catchphrase rotation or evidence that these events happened in a save.

## Relationships at work

**Erin:** a potential mentor. He admires her command of the office and may initially assume she never struggles. A useful friendship develops when she shows him how to clarify a request, protect a lunch break and leave a readable handover. She does not automatically become his supervisor, caretaker or romantic partner.

**Richard:** Michael wants his approval and is vulnerable to an open-ended "one more thing." Richard values useful work and may lean on that eagerness, consistent with his existing managerial characterization. Michael's growth gives him increasingly clear ways to ask Richard to choose a priority. A refusal is handled through the real assignment system and does not invent an automatic punishment.

**Daniel:** practical coaching about asking for the deadline and intended recipient before starting. Daniel's competitive approach can motivate Michael, but can also make him feel he ought to keep volunteering.

**Nadia:** teaches him to distinguish a helpful question from a failure. She values a checked source and a clear handoff more than a rushed yes. She may be blunt about a mistake while accepting the correction.

**Peter Walsh:** a proposed experienced colleague who gives a small, concrete tip and then lets Michael do the task. He does not silently complete the intern's objectives or grant unearned credit.

## Work habits and actual task rules

Michael's role is junior intern; he is not Erin's secretary role with a different name. He runs documents, checks versions, counts supplies, makes authorized copies, updates contact details and helps set up meetings. Complex or confidential work requires actual authorization and supervision.

His cubicle stays at M, reached from Sales Cubicles H. Proposed personal details: a notebook with crossed-out first attempts, a repaired desk lamp, one small cooperative-game token and a handwritten reminder to check the destination before carrying a document. Each belongs beneath his workstation, not in the top-level room action list. Their appearance does not make them free inventory.

When presented with work, he checks three facts: requested output, due time and current commitments. Early in his story he may voice an immediate desire to help, but accepting an assignment still uses the actual player/authority controls. The game must not accept a task or spend time solely because an AI line says "sure."

Overload is based on actual incomplete commitments and deadlines. It must not be a random excuse. A conflict offers useful choices: ask which comes first, negotiate a later deadline, decline the new work or finish an already accepted obligation. There is no hidden rule that every task must be accepted to keep him happy.

## Learning to set boundaries

Use remembered episodes, rather than making personality a single obedience meter.

1. **Wants to prove himself:** enthusiastic about a first useful assignment. He needs a nudge to check whether its deadline fits.
2. **Learns clarification:** after an actual confusing handoff, he starts checking which document/version/person was intended.
3. **Names the conflict:** he recognizes two incompatible requests and asks a lead to choose.
4. **Makes an honest offer:** he offers a feasible part of the work or a later time.
5. **Keeps a boundary:** he can decline overload calmly and still be friendly and helpful the following day.

These are personal growth beats, not automatic daily upgrades. Progress requires relevant completed conversations/actions and persists after reload. A quiet day does not erase it. Do not award repeated relationship or mastery progress for clicking the same coaching answer. Boundary confidence remains separate from form influence, mastery and relationship; none substitutes for another.

He can still have a difficult day after learning to say no. Show it through brief hesitation and an available choice, not a reset to total helplessness. Helpful feedback must not become a universal minigame where every NPC repeats a lesson.

## As an active form

The same earnest adult is present internally. His body and voice are his; the original player's consciousness is within that embodied identity rather than standing alongside him. Internal speech can reveal uncertainty that he would hide from Richard. It must not be exposed to other NPCs as spoken words.

His form-led impulses may favor arriving on time, finishing a promised delivery or avoiding embarrassment. A failed resistance can select only a legal action permitted by the shared authority engine. Michael's desire to be useful is not authorization to accept unlimited work, spend unlimited money, reveal private thoughts or grant an NPC control outside those rules.

Cooperative exchanges can involve agreeing on a realistic plan: "Let's deliver this and then take lunch." The plan must have real steps, completion memory and an end. It cannot turn into a permanent work obligation. Body needs, fatigue, current room, available stock and the workday clock must affect what is feasible.

Permanent mastery preserves his personality and remembered growth. It does not erase him or make him agree to every intention. Actual action authority follows mastered-form rules, with no automatic influence regain merely because he expresses a different preference. His unmastered influence cannot be lowered just by rewriting an AI response.

An unequipped form does not independently walk through the city or meet friends. Before adding a separate world Michael NPC, explicitly resolve the NPC/form lifecycle so there are no two Michaels with contradictory memories or simultaneous physical presence. This design does not quietly decide that unresolved world rule.

## Daily rhythm

Proposed weekday routine: wash and dress; prepare a simple breakfast and available lunch; check the day's commitments; commute through legal routes; work 09:00–17:00 with an actual lunch break; leave a useful handover; return home; eat, play a game or work on a small repair; prepare for sleep. Weekends permit a slower start, groceries, a hobby and occasional company.

These are character intentions until Michael's home, wardrobe, food stock and routes exist. Do not use Erin's private apartment or belongings as his default household. Do not display a completed shower, meal, commute or repair if the engine has only announced an intention.

## Acceptance criteria for implementation

- Direct questions receive relevant short answers; no repeated apologies or robotic deference.
- Michael can ask for clarification without being treated as refusing all work.
- A real task conflict exposes reprioritization and refusal; dialogue alone accepts no task.
- Growth is earned through relevant events and survives reload without trust farming.
- Praise does not bypass choices, boundaries or form authority.
- Work and body needs produce legal physical actions and completion records.
- Richard, Erin and the leads remember only completed shared events; private thoughts stay private.
- Mastery, relationship, influence and boundary confidence remain distinct.
- Michael has no independent offscreen existence while inactive as a form, and no unreviewed NPC duplicate is spawned.
