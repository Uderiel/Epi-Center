# Epi-Center Combat Foundations — Guide and Eve Dialogue Reference

Status: foundation prototype; no live combat interface yet. This document is the source for future Guide entries and Eve's explanations. Exact balance values are provisional.

## What is a combatant?
Players, NPCs, forms and cursed objects use the same basic record: identity, type, rarity, ten attributes, Health, Stamina, Resolve, Arousal, abilities, conditions, equipment, relationships and form state. Each individual may have different resource maximums and special actions. Rarity sets a baseline difficulty, not an automatic win.

## What do the four bars mean?
- Health: physical survival. Zero can mean physical defeat.
- Stamina: effort and exertion. Exhaustion does not automatically mean defeat.
- Resolve: mental resilience and capacity to resist influence. Zero can mean mental defeat.
- Arousal: an internal pressure meter. Reaching its encounter-defined maximum can be a loss condition.
Willpower and Stability are attributes for checks; Resolve is a resource, not an eleventh attribute.

## What happens when I am captured or encased?
Capture is not necessarily the end of play. Look for ways to recover Resolve and attempt mental saving throws, while also seeking opportunities to escape physically. The prototype has a Resist action that restores a small amount of Resolve on success and an Escape action that reduces restraint/encasement. The exact action economy and capture-specific difficulty modifiers are not finalized. Recovering Resolve improves the ability to withstand sustained mental pressure; it does not directly add a bonus to a d20 saving throw.

## What does a saving throw do?
A saving throw is a d20 roll plus a relevant attribute and modifiers against a difficulty target. It can resist hypnosis, influence, restraint or other effects. The prototype uses natural 20 as success and natural 1 as failure.

## What if a form wins?
The current encounter records defeat, but defeat is not permanent loss of agency or mastery potential. A form may gain influence over routines or actions as defined by future consequence rules. The player can resist internally, escape and try again. A form's victory does not automatically complete all 23 transformation stages or replace original attributes.

## What is mastery?
Mastery can eventually follow overcoming a form, including from within its influence. Mastery tiers and exact progress rules are not yet designed. Do not award mastery automatically from a single prototype combat result.

## What does the engine save?
Combat runs on an isolated snapshot. Its outcome record states who won, how combatants ended, and what occurred. The engine does not automatically alter persistent player or form data. A later consequence system will interpret approved outcomes.

## What can Eve explain?
Suggested future dialogue intents: `combat.basics`, `combat.resources`, `combat.saving_throws`, `combat.capture`, `combat.form_influence`, `combat.mastery`. Eve should use the same explanations as this Guide, with short examples and no new mechanics.

## Automatic combat alignment
No player or form chooses a specialization manually. Physical = Fitness + Endurance + Reflexes + 0.5 Intuition; Mental = Willpower + Reasoning + Stability + 0.5 Insight; Arousal = Influence + Presence + Insight + 0.5 Intuition. Each score divided by the sum of all three scores gives its percentage. Equal leaders are Hybrid, all-zero is Unassigned. Alignment measures allocation, not raw combat power. The Character screen and Stats drawer show a three-segment alignment bar and score breakdown. Future form/NPC/cursed-object profiles use the same function. Matchup modifiers remain provisional.

## Approved encounter action and reaction decisions (2026-10-28)
Status: **approved design rules; not yet integrated into the live Play encounter interface or engine.** Do not confuse this section with implemented mechanics. User confirmed Android combat alignment display in character creation Save Slot 2 and character stats Save Slot 1.

- **Flexible turn order (Option B):** Each combatant has one main action and one minor action per turn, usable in either order. Either may be skipped. Reaction allowance is separate.
- **Hybrid defense (Option C):** Routine attacks check passive defense; a combatant may elect to spend an eligible reaction on active defense. Proposed safety rule: effective defense = max(passive defense, active defense roll), so a poor active roll cannot lower protection. Exact passive-defense formulas, tie resolution and bonuses remain provisional.
- **Reaction allowance:** Normally one reaction per round, shared among defense, counterattack and explicitly reaction-tagged special abilities. This is the recommended architecture pending final engine integration.
- **Juri / Counter Attack:** The foe's initial attack resolves completely first, hit or miss. Juri may then retaliate using her reaction if she remains capable and other prerequisites are satisfied. The legacy +5 Strength attack bonus must be mapped into the ten-attribute system without silently converting it.
- **Juri / Athletic Save:** Tier-four, once per encounter. When a physical attack would hit, spend the available reaction and the encounter use to roll 1d6. Results 4, 5, 6 negate damage and on-hit effects from **one incoming attack instance**; results 1, 2, 3 fail and the attack resolves normally. The use is expended on attempt even if the roll fails. Multi-hit attacks are not wholly canceled. Does not remove pre-existing effects. Does not protect against mental or arousal attacks by default.
- **Forms and abilities:** Distinguish allocated base attributes, independent form attributes, passive modifiers, activated main/minor abilities, reaction abilities, and temporary encounter conditions. Combat Alignment describes attribute distribution, not an ability restriction. Do not replace the base character's attributes until the established 23-stage transformation requirement is completed.
- **Legacy examples for later conversion:** Neko Cafe form (roll-enhancing d6); Street Fighter Juri (counter and emergency save); Nature Dryad (two root attacks every third turn and limited transformation-buildup protection); Lolita Maid (encounter-duration cognitive debuff); Bimbo's Seductress (once-per-encounter ally extra attack). Preserve original legacy stat and skill meanings pending explicit six-to-ten attribute mapping. Do not equate extra attack with extra full turn.
- **Proposed ability record model:** trigger -> eligibility -> cost -> roll -> effects -> duration -> cleanup. Abilities should be registered as data and shown contextually in the mobile encounter interface, with available reactions shown during enemy turns and main/minor choices on the player's turn.

## Approved encounter-only transformation buildup (2026-10-28)
Status: approved design architecture; numeric buildup rates and resistance checks provisional; not implemented in live combat.
- Encounter Transformation Buildup is a temporary combat-only meter, separate from the existing 23-stage free-roam transformation progression.
- Track buildup independently per transformation source and target; do not automatically pool different opponents' progress. Cooperative effects require explicit ability definitions.
- A successful transformation ability may add buildup, with a proposed normalized 0-100 meter. Failure adds none unless ability explicitly states otherwise. Exact amounts are not approved.
- Defenses, escape, specialized protections, and ally actions can prevent or reduce buildup according to ability rules. Define counterplay and limits before implementation.
- At threshold, resolve encounter-specific capture/transformation opportunity, not automatic permanent replacement of player attributes or advancement of free-roam stages.
- At encounter end, discard temporary buildup tracks; persist only separately approved consequence records (e.g., temporary capture or influence), never implicit transformation-stage changes.
- Resolve simultaneous sources, repeated attempts, protection duration and encounter-end cleanup deterministically; numerical balancing remains open.

## Final encounter transformation resistance (approved 2026-10-28)
Status: approved design; exact DC and reduction value provisional; no engine implementation yet.
- When a source-target encounter-only transformation buildup track reaches its threshold (proposed 100), the defender receives a final resistance check before the encounter-specific transformation objective succeeds.
- This final check is available even when the defender has already spent the normal reaction; it is a threshold safeguard, not a reaction ability.
- On success, prevent the immediate transformation objective and reduce only that source-target buildup substantially; 50/100 is a provisional playtest reset value.
- On failure, resolve the source's defined encounter-specific transformation consequence. Do not automatically advance persistent 23-stage free-roam transformation progress or replace base character attributes.
- Resistance attribute, DC, modifiers, limits on repeated threshold checks, special immunity, simultaneous thresholds and subsequent escape/rescue rules remain to be approved.

## Approved defender-choice transformation resistance (2026-10-28)
Status: approved design rule, not implemented in the live encounter engine.
- Each transformation ability defines its eligible resistance attributes, ordinarily two thematically appropriate choices; the defender chooses one when making the final transformation resistance check.
- Physical assimilation example: Endurance or Fitness. Mental conditioning: Willpower or Stability. Arousal-driven transformation: Stability or Willpower. Unusual effects explicitly define their own eligible attributes.
- Do not let attacks automatically target the defender's weakest attribute, and do not allow unrestricted choice among all ten attributes.
- Display both allowed attributes and current bonuses, including relevant conditions, in the mobile resistance prompt. Resolve one check using the selected attribute.
- Meaningful choice can arise from ability-specific secondary consequences, resource costs and conditions, but exact modifiers, DCs, and numeric balancing are not yet approved.

## Encased-player contested control proposal (2026-10-28)
Status: design discussion; user proposed control checks and influence/arousal-scaled difficulty. Numeric DC and turn-action rules are NOT yet approved or implemented.
- An encased player may have physical options restricted while Mental or Arousal resistance remains available.
- At the encased player's turn, propose one control-resistance roll with an eligible Mental or Arousal attribute. Success reduces encasing-form influence and preserves the player's offensive action; failure lets the encasing form direct one offensive action for that turn, not the entire turn.
- The player should retain visible status, combat log and eligible defense/escape actions. Encasing form must respect action eligibility and resource limits; control is temporary and contestable.
- Influence percentage should be primary control difficulty driver; arousal percentage a smaller secondary modifier. Suggested playtest DC: 14 + 2*floor(influence/25) + (arousal>=75 ? 2 : arousal>=50 ? 1 : 0). This is PROVISIONAL; exact scaling, boundary at 100%, and base DC need approval.
- Use selected ten-attribute modifier for the resistance roll; combat alignment percentages are descriptive, not substitute modifiers.
- Open decisions: when influence increases, how much successful resistance removes, whether one roll covers both channels, how controlled offensive action is selected and resolved in multiplayer, and escape while encased.

## Approved controlled-offense and archetype parity (2026-10-28)
Status: user-approved design direction; not yet implemented in live combat.
- When an encased player fails their Mental or Arousal control-resistance check, the encasing form may direct ONE eligible offensive action during that player's turn, including targeting the player's allies.
- Control of that action is temporary; it does not grant control over the entire turn, defensive reactions, resistance choices, escape opportunities, or permanent allegiance.
- The form-directed offensive action must respect the character's action economy, available resources, legal targets, and applicable conditions. Exact selection and multiplayer handling remain unresolved.
- Physical, Mental, and Arousal specialties have distinct situational advantages and disadvantages across approach, containment, transformation buildup, influence struggle, and recovery; no archetype dominates all stages.
- Specialty/Combat Alignment is a descriptive guideline derived from attribute allocation, not a restriction, override, or substitute for individual ten-attribute scores. Actual attributes, abilities, conditions, and rolls resolve actions.
- Stage-based options are design goals, not free automatic bonuses; define concrete abilities and counters before implementation.

## Approved controlled-action player engagement (2026-10-28)
Status: approved design rule; not yet implemented in live encounter UI or engine.
- During temporary encasement control, the captured character retains their existing initiative slot and turn rotation; the controller does not gain a second turn through them.
- At the controlled character's turn, offer the eligible Mental or Arousal resistance check. Success preserves independent offensive action and reduces influence. Failure grants the encasing form target selection for ONE offensive action, including an ally target.
- On a failed resistance check, the player still chooses which legal offensive technique their character uses against the controller-selected target. Resolve with the character's actual stats, abilities, costs, conditions, and remaining action allowance.
- Explicit ability text may exceptionally dictate the technique as well as the target; such exceptions must be authored and documented, not assumed.
- Preserve the player's access to permitted defensive reactions, resistance choices and escape options. Do not create a bonus action or extra turn for the controlling form.
- Still unresolved: how to handle no legal offensive technique, target becoming invalid, voluntary resource spending, ability cooldowns, and the exact influence gain/loss and resistance DC formula.

## Approved safeguards for controlled offensive actions (2026-10-28)
Status: approved design rules; not yet implemented in live encounter engine or UI.
- A controller cannot compel expenditure of a captured character's limited-use abilities, consumables, or optional resources. The player may voluntarily choose a legal technique that spends those resources.
- The controller selects a legal target by default, while the player chooses the eligible offensive technique. Explicitly authored exceptional control abilities require separate review and must not silently bypass resource safeguards.
- If no legal offensive technique is available, forfeit the controlled offensive action; do not fabricate an attack, bypass cooldowns, spend resources involuntarily, or grant extra actions.
- Keep the captured character's original initiative slot, eligible defenses, resistance and escape opportunities; no bonus turn for the controlling form.
- Future implementation should test empty action menus, depleted resources, cooldowns, target invalidation, ally targeting, and action-economy invariants.

## Approved defensive attribute pairings (2026-10-28)
Status: attribute pairings approved; numeric formulas and hit probabilities remain provisional and unimplemented.
- Physical Defense: primary Reflexes, secondary Endurance.
- Mental Defense: primary Willpower, secondary Reasoning.
- Arousal Defense: primary Stability, secondary Insight.
- Candidate passive defense formula for each: 10 + primary attribute + floor(secondary attribute / 2). Base 10 and half weighting require simulation before approval.
- Candidate attack resolution: 1d20 + attack-specific attribute + applicable modifiers versus the targeted defense. Meeting the defense succeeds is proposed, not yet approved.
- Candidate balance target: approximately 50-60% ordinary attack hit chance against comparably developed defenders; test cross-specialty mismatches and active reactions before committing.

## Initial defense accuracy simulation (2026-10-28)
Status: analytical playtest only; no formula or rarity progression approved or implemented.

- Proposed attack attribute used for tests: Physical Fitness, Mental Influence, Arousal Presence. Passive defense = 10 + primary + floor(secondary/2). Hypothetical rarity adds +2 attack and +2 passive defense per tier; not a production rarity rule.
- Equal-rarity hit chances by attacking archetype Physical: versus Physical 40%, Mental 70%, Arousal 70%; Mental: versus Physical 70%, Mental 40%, Arousal 70%; Arousal: versus Physical 70%, Mental 65%, Arousal 40%.
- Same-specialty hit chance versus passive defense base 7/8/9/10/11: 55/50/45/40/35 percent respectively.
- Rarity gap +/-1 tier changes hit chance by 10 percentage points under the hypothetical +2/tier model. Do not assume rarity boosts both attack and defense in final design.
- Exhaustive 400-pair d20 test of active defense max(passive, 1d20+primary) for primary=7, secondary=3, attack bonus=7: base 8 passive hit 50%, active hit 38.75%; base 10 passive hit 40%, active hit 33%.

Test broader allocation distributions, action economy, damage, resource costs, final transformation resistance and party composition before approving numbers.

## Living faction retaliation, mission failure, and difficulty sequencing (2026-10-28)
Status: user-approved world-design principles. Response-state names and implementation specifics below are provisional proposals, not engine changes.
- The world is living: NPCs and factions can observe, plan, adapt, strategize and choose responses, just as players can. No automatic omniscience: retaliation should depend on what the faction plausibly witnessed, discovered, or attributed.
- Failed cylinder retrieval: surviving players may retreat, regroup and retry. Attempts can trigger defensive reinforcement/recalibration, new traps, barricaded entrances, altered patrols, revoked safe passage, faction hostility, and temporary cylinder concealment/relocation under stronger guard. Consequences persist appropriately across missions and player encounters.
- Possible reusable faction alert ladder (PROPOSAL): unaware, suspicious, alerted, hostile, lockdown. Each faction expresses response through its own lore/theme (e.g. living slime passages vs mecha access-control and scanners); not all factions must use identical stages or thresholds.
- Cylinder mission is a multi-encounter operation with infiltration, retrieval, extraction and attrition; defeating one group does not defeat a whole faction. Track party Health/resources, alarms, pursuit, access, objective location and persistent faction relations between stages. Avoid arbitrary unavoidable ambushes or unknowable punishment; telegraph risk and allow meaningful counterplay.
- Mission difficulty and reward/value should scale with threat, mission sequencing, location, number of stages, accumulated attrition and consequence severity, not merely single-fight stat inflation. Safe quests are direct, simpler, fewer stages; higher-reward missions can require longer, riskier sequences and repeated encounters.
- Anti-speedrun design: allow earned efficiency from mastery/gear/tactics, but faction response, mission objectives and attrition provide sustainable challenge. Do not artificially equalize every party's win chance or secretly hard-counter its forms.
- Follow-up design decisions to resolve: faction knowledge/evidence tracking, individual vs party reputation, alert escalation/de-escalation, retreat and recovery, cylinder relocation rules, route blockade counterplay, mission save/persistence, attrition and difficulty bands.

## Approved: defense versus mitigation separation (2026-10-28)
Status: user-approved design; documentation only, no live engine changes.
- Defense determines whether an attack/effect hits or connects; mitigation determines the impact that passes through after a successful hit. Nimble/evasive combatants may have high defense and low mitigation; tank/protective forms may have moderate defense and high mitigation.
- Physical protection, Mental resilience, and Arousal resistance are separate mitigation categories. Transformation-specific resistance/protection remains explicitly identifiable and is not automatically covered by arousal resistance.
- Mitigation primarily arises from forms, equipment, conditions, and authored abilities, rather than duplicating the attributes that already increase passive defense. Every effect defines which mitigation applies; protection does not universally prevent unrelated effects, control, capture, or buildup.
- Still to decide: flat versus proportional reduction, stacking and penetration, minimum damage, multi-effect mitigation order, protection durability and exceptions.

## Approved: hybrid mitigation and ordering (2026-10-28)
Status: user-approved design; documentation only, not implemented in live combat.
- After attack resolution, apply only mitigation applicable to each explicitly authored effect.
- Hybrid mitigation supports flat damage reduction, percentage reduction, and effect-specific protection (e.g. transformation buildup or control where defined).
- Apply flat reduction FIRST, then percentage reduction; floor remaining ordinary damage to an integer and clamp minimum to zero. Example: 30 Physical damage, 5 flat reduction, 20% reduction => floor((30-5)*0.8)=20 damage.
- A protection against Physical damage does not automatically prevent Mental/Arousal damage, transformation buildup, control, capture or other separate effects.
- Numeric amounts, stacking/caps, penetration, minimum damage exceptions, mitigation durability, and multiple simultaneous protection interactions remain unresolved pending tests.

## Approved baseline: mitigation stacking and targeted penetration (2026-10-28)
Status: user accepts this framework subject to balance validation; documentation only, no live engine change.
- Compatible flat reductions add together. Independent compatible percentage reductions combine multiplicatively, not by adding percentages: 20% and 25% => 1-(0.8*0.75)=40% total reduction.
- Penetration affects only explicitly identified mitigation categories or layers; no universal bypass of unrelated Physical, Mental, Arousal, or transformation protections.
- Avoid accidental 100% immunity from ordinary gear/mastery stacking. True immunity requires an expressly authored exceptional effect.
- Duplicate effect compatibility, diminishing returns, temporary shield interactions, percentage caps, minimum damage, and penetration strength are still PROVISIONAL and must be stress-tested across optimized and ordinary builds, rarities, forms and gear.
- Player preference: choose the most balanced system based on testing rather than needless complexity or arbitrary caps; preserve meaningful tank, evasion, support and specialist playstyles.

## Approved: natural 1, natural 20, and ability-defined critical effects (2026-10-28)
Status: user-approved combat resolution design; documentation only, not live implementation.
- On an attack d20, natural 1 is automatic failure regardless of modifiers, with no automatic catastrophic mishap.
- On an attack d20, natural 20 is automatic success and triggers the attack ability's explicitly authored critical benefit.
- Critical effects are ability-defined, not universal double damage. For ordinary damaging attacks, adding one extra damage die is a provisional default to test, not yet a locked damage formula.
- Transformation/control critical effects may enhance pressure or conditions but cannot bypass mandatory final transformation resistance, control-resistance safeguards, or automatically cause permanent character changes.
- Test critical frequency and mitigation interactions across ordinary, hybrid and optimized builds; clarify whether these natural-roll rules apply to non-attack checks separately.

## Approved shared encounter resolution: remove opposition, not necessarily HP (2026-10-28)
User clarifies fundamental victory model. Physical removes foes via knockout, killing or destruction. Mental can incapacitate, confuse or redirect foes against one another. Arousal can lower guards/focus, create openings and—through appropriate authored abilities—create allies. Transformation, primarily a threat to player parties, can whittle down the party by removing or converting characters according to authored encounter outcomes. Not every enemy must die; an encounter can resolve when opposition is removed or no longer able/willing to contest the objective.
- Track active, impaired, temporarily neutralized, and defeated/out-of-fight states; specify what authored effect causes each and when temporary outcomes expire or can be reversed. Confusion, arousal pressure, buildup or partial control alone are not automatically removal.
- Distinguish combat removal from durable allegiance, capture, persistent transformation and permanent consequences. Do not auto-override player identity or bypass final transformation resistance and control safeguards.
- Victory evaluator must consider side/allegiance changes, incapacitation, retreat, surrender, effective control of objective, and recoverable neutralization; avoid HP-only elimination bias.
- Arousal can contribute indirectly through debuffs and openings without having to provide solo instant defeat. Mental redirection can change effective side pressure without requiring HP damage. Transformation pressure has source-specific tracks and explicit outcomes.
- Future simulations must model time-to-neutralization, duration/reversal, action economy, team synergy, objective completion, and faction/location-specific mixes. No numerical thresholds approved in this note.

## Critical design correction: encounter defeat is not game failure (2026-10-28)
USER-APPROVED INTENT: Epi-Center is a transformation-focused text-adventure roleplay RPG, not a conventional death/quest-success optimization game. Players may deliberately seek defeat, capture, transformation, faction allegiance, and adversarial roles; these may be enjoyable, desirable and meaningful progression. Combat winner, mission objective result, narrative consequence, and player-valued outcome are distinct. An encounter loss can launch captivity, escape, faction recruitment, new forms, relationships, alternate missions, resistance, betrayal, rescue, or opposing other players. A transformed player may become the type of creature that defeated them and participate in that faction's activities. Preserve playable continuation and agency; don't force automatic reset or label every capture/transformation as a bad ending.
- Model encounters as **branching state transitions**, not binary win/loss terminal states. Separate tactical result, objective state (completed/blocked/changed/abandoned), faction/relationship changes, player condition/form, freedom/captivity, and unlocked follow-on choices. Explicitly distinguish temporary and durable consequences and the means to escape/reverse/negotiate/embrace them.
- Three combat disciplines and transformation remain distinct, with natural strengths, weaknesses and counterplay. No requirement that all archetypes can win all encounters, nor that every mission has equal completion odds. Risk and enemy strength depend on lore, role, tier, depth, faction knowledge, and player actions.
- Player intent matters: record optional goal/preferences for win, surrender, capture, acquisition of a form, faction recruitment, escape or subversion, without scripting a guaranteed outcome. Do not treat voluntary transformation/capture as failure by default. Keep consent and safety/agency controls distinct from in-fiction character coercion.
- PvP and faction loyalty: transformed/recruited players can oppose other players if the game's PvP rules and consent/participation settings allow. NPC faction AI must track knowledge/evidence rather than omniscience. Preserve ongoing player identity and save continuity.
- Quest evaluation should measure **branch reachability and meaningful continuation**, not maximize the rate of combat victories. Useful metrics: reachable endings and follow-up quests, escape opportunities, time spent without agency, softlocks, unfair forced states, continuity of inventory/form/relationships, exploit loops, coherence of NPC response, and whether players who pursue distinct goals can make progress. Test both deliberate defeat and ordinary defeat, and replayable post-capture/post-transformation paths.
- Prior 201,600 / 7,680 / 38,400 / 29,160 trials were limited tactical stress tests. Their quest-success percentages must NOT be used as overall game quality or balance targets. Do not automatically retune encounters to equalize those percentages. Next engineering task: branching outcome graph, explicit captivity/transformation/faction states, escape and alternative quest entry points, and tests for nonterminal outcomes before more broad win-rate optimization.

## Owner-approved action classification and influence continuation (2026-10-28)
Status: APPROVED DESIGN; not yet implemented in the combat engine or live Play. Numeric costs/DCs and per-ability exceptions remain pending.
- Universal classification by action PURPOSE, not by which of the ten attributes is rolled: Main = outward-directed/offensive action affecting another target or environment (Physical, Mental, Arousal, grapple, encase, etc.); Minor = inward/self-directed action (resist influence, regain stability/composure, self-focused restraint/encasement escape). The same attribute may be used for either category depending on the action. Passive/automatic saving throws are not Minor actions. Triggered active defenses and counterattacks remain Reactions.
- Approved escape classification: self-directed attempt to break restraints or encasement is Minor, not automatically Main. Successful escape may leave Main available, subject to conditions and resources.
- Failed Minor resistance against a controlling form does NOT automatically consume the character's Main action or end their turn. In line with previously approved control rules, the form may direct that Main offensive action at a legal target, including an ally; the player chooses a legal offensive technique and it resolves using their actual attributes, abilities, costs, and conditions. The controller does not acquire the whole turn or a bonus turn, cannot compel optional/limited-use resources or consumables, and cannot override eligible reactions/escape/resistance. If no legal offensive technique exists, the controlled offensive action is forfeited.
- Pending precise design: which influence/encasement checks are automatic triggers versus voluntary Minor actions, interaction with multiple simultaneous conditions, action eligibility after escape, and exceptional authored abilities. Do not silently finalize these details.

## Approved two-stage control resistance and Minor follow-up (2026-10-28)
Status: OWNER-APPROVED DESIGN; not yet implemented or balance-tested.
- Stage 1: an incoming/ongoing control effect triggers an automatic defensive resistance check at the controlled character's turn. This consumes neither Main nor Minor. On success, the player retains target selection for their Main action; on failure, the influencing form temporarily selects a legal target, potentially an ally.
- Stage 2: the player may use their Minor action to respond. Following automatic success, Minor may be used to actively reduce remaining influence. Following automatic failure, Minor may be used for a second resistance attempt to regain control of target selection before Main resolves. On second success, player chooses target; on second failure, form's legal target remains and player chooses the legal offensive technique. Other eligible Minor choices (e.g. escape restraint) compete with this resistance option.
- Preserve prior safeguards: one Main and one Minor per turn, separate reaction; no bonus turn for controller; no forced optional/limited-use expenditure; if no legal technique exists, forfeit controlled offensive action. Exact DCs, influence reduction amounts for each check, and ordering with unusual conditions remain provisional pending tests.

## Approved two-stage control resistance (2026-10-28)
Status: OWNER APPROVED design; NOT yet implemented or numerically balanced.
- When a form's influence attempts to control a character's offensive target, first resolve an automatic, condition-triggered resistance check without spending Main or Minor.
- On SUCCESS, the character retains their own target selection. They may voluntarily spend their Minor action to further reduce remaining influence immediately; otherwise Minor remains available for another legal self-directed action.
- On FAILURE, the influencing form temporarily gains target selection for the character's Main offensive action, including legal ally targets. The player may spend their Minor action attempting to regain control of target selection. If that Minor attempt succeeds, the player selects the target; if it fails or is skipped, the form selects the target. The player still selects the legal offensive technique in all cases, subject to prior resource/agency safeguards.
- Failure does not force spending the Minor action. No extra turn, forced consumables or optional limited-use expenditure. If no legal offensive technique is available, the controlled offensive action is forfeited.
- Exact resistance DCs, influence reductions, and timing relative to other conditions remain pending balancing/implementation. Do not infer guaranteed influence reduction from an automatic successful check beyond previously approved provisional values.

## Approved encounter transformation, composite capture and entity-authoring requirements (2026-10-28)
Status: OWNER-APPROVED DESIGN, NOT IMPLEMENTED OR BALANCE-TESTED. This section supplements earlier action/control decisions. Do not conflate illustrative examples with numeric approvals.

### Transformation attack and live encounter feed
- An encounter transformation source (e.g. Latex Panther) may make a hit roll against the appropriate defense. Margin of success unlocks a CHOICE of eligible initial body-contact locations: narrow success permits less consequential locations; greater success permits more strategically vital locations. The attacking entity selects among unlocked locations according to its authored tactics. Exact margins and body-part ranking are per-ability/encounter design, not yet approved.
- Track per-source, per-target temporary encounter transformation buildup PLUS anatomical progression: initial contact location, direction of spread, affected regions, visual changes, and functional limitations. Buildup percentage is not just a bar. A transformed hand/paw may disable gripping an axe; equipment/action eligibility updates from actual anatomy. The source defines buildup and progression; shared combat/equipment rules enforce effects. Do not equate temporary buildup with Erin's persistent 23-stage cursed-item progression.
- Erin's red heels are a CURSED-ITEM transformation source, not a standard hostile form encounter. Masks, skinsuits, cursed clothing, jewelry, slime/goo attacks and encasing creatures may activate differently while sharing anatomy/effects infrastructure.
- The encounter live feed advances combatant-by-combatant and turn-by-turn: show automatic checks (reason, roll, DC, result), status and anatomy updates, chosen Main/Minor in either order, reactions in their timing windows, attack accuracy/margin, rolled damage or authored effects, costs, and grounded flavor narration that reflects the actual engine outcome (near miss, barely hit, strong hit, transformed body part). Continue until tactical resolution or a branching continuation state. No invented rolls or effects in narration.

### Restraint, influence and ally assistance
- Physical restraint/encasement and Mental influence are INDEPENDENT progressing states. Physical restraint progressively restricts movement, physical attack accuracy/damage and applicable equipment/abilities; it must not indiscriminately penalize nonphysical abilities. Mental influence progressively contests selection of the Main action's target, not automatic control of the whole turn. Numeric penalty curves and thresholds remain open.
- An ally may spend a legal outward-directed Main action (or an explicitly authored alternative ability/reaction) to reduce restraint or encounter transformation buildup by an ability-defined amount, rather than automatically ripping off a whole encasing creature. Meaningful reversal and equipment recovery depend on actual region states. One character's Minor action may be contested between internal escape and regaining targeting control.
- Contact/splash slime or goo transformation can affect multiple targets independently without merging combatants; separate transformation buildup and anatomical effects per source/target. Encasement/absorption is a distinct ability with capacity constraints.

### Latex Panther single-captive composite combatant
- While physically encasing a target, the Panther's own combat effectiveness is progressively reduced by its commitment to capture; precise penalties need simulation. The captive remains separately acting while partial encasement rules apply.
- Once absorption completes, Panther plus captured player become ONE EXTERNAL COMBATANT with ONE Main and ONE Minor action, not two independently acting units or two action pools.
