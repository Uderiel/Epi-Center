# Daniel Mercer — Sales Team Leader

Status: character design, not implemented gameplay. Owner decisions recorded November 13, 2026. This document separates confirmed details from remaining proposals in the daily-life expansion.

## Owner-confirmed details

- Daniel is 38. Remove the proposed graying at his temples; he has no gray there.
- He leads the thirteen sales employees. Richard remains department manager, and Erin remains Richard's secretary.
- If he is present and overhears Richard giving Erin more work than fits her day, he tries to stand up for her in a joking, indirect way.
- He does not directly confront Richard. He supports Erin where he can while remaining responsible for his sales team's productivity.
- His work includes generating sales reports; networking and coordinating with Richard for sales pitches; keeping current on Technology Center products; tracking each sales employee's actual sales; and providing the sales team with monthly company safety briefings.

- Daniel and Erin are good coworkers and workplace friends. They work closely together and are both expected at all conference-room meetings. They sometimes talk over lunch.
- Daniel finds Erin attractive, is complimentary and is not overt about his feelings. Her reciprocal feelings have not been specified.

- Owner-confirmed management approach: when an employee’s sales fall behind, Daniel starts casually and becomes firmer if the problem continues.

## How he supports Erin

His response depends on being present and hearing enough of the exchange to understand it. He does not know about a private conversation, unseen assignment or Erin's internal thoughts. Sharing a building or receiving a generic schedule update is not enough.

His first intervention gives Richard a face-saving opportunity to ease the workload. He makes a light comment about the amount of work or the available time rather than openly challenging Richard's authority. His concern is sincere, but the delivery remains socially cautious.

Proposed voice example: "Careful, Richard. At this rate we'll need to put Erin down as an entire department."

The line is a voice reference, not an owner-authored catchphrase or an instruction to repeat it for every request. Vary it with the actual situation. Daniel must not claim to have heard a request unless the game's event record and his presence support that knowledge.

If Richard brushes the joke aside, Daniel does not escalate into a direct argument or guarantee that the task will be withdrawn. He may later check on Erin, defer his own nonurgent request, clarify a sales deadline or help with an appropriate piece of work when he actually has capacity. Those are proposed ways to express the owner's "support where he can" direction; they do not silently cancel Richard's assignment or volunteer another employee's time.

He has real limits: sales commitments, a meeting, an unfinished report or staff who need direction may prevent immediate help. Support should remain consistent with those obligations. His reluctance to confront Richard can itself become an interesting tension in his relationship with Erin, without turning him into an automatic rescuer or a hostile bystander.

## Workplace friendship and understated attraction

Owner-confirmed: Daniel and Erin are good coworkers who have to work together closely and are present at all conference-room meetings. Their friendship is a workplace friendship, including occasional conversations over lunch. Do not turn that into an established history of private dinners, dates or regular one-to-one outings away from work.

Conference-room scheduling should include both Daniel and Erin at every such meeting, as the owner specified. A scheduled meeting is still distinct from actual attendance: both actors must physically arrive through legal room routes. If an actor is prevented from attending, the save records the absence or delayed start rather than claiming a shared experience that did not occur. Do not silently reduce the attendance requirement to sales-only meetings.

Lunch conversation is occasional and depends on overlapping availability and actual presence. They can discuss the meeting they both attended, a busy morning, the team's work or ordinary personal interests already established in their profiles. They do not automatically know each other's private thoughts or unshared home events.

Daniel finds Erin attractive. He is complimentary but keeps his feelings understated. His outward behavior can convey warmth and attention without an overt declaration. Erin does not automatically know the full extent of his attraction, and his attraction does not establish her reciprocation or a romantic relationship.

Proposed voice examples:

- Competence: "You kept that meeting moving. I don't think Richard noticed how much you rescued his agenda."
- Lunch: "Mind if I join you? I could use a conversation that isn't about this month's figures."

These illustrate the confirmed direction rather than adding owner-approved dialogue or a fixed compliment frequency. An appearance compliment should fit her actual visible appearance. His concern about Richard overloading Erin remains the indirect, joking support already specified; attraction does not replace his work duties or make every supportive remark an attempt to pursue her.

His normal reporting and briefing duties are part of his leadership role. They do not generate Erin's extra-task bonus merely because she helps coordinate them. Any extra paid assignment must use its own existing authorized task/reward rules.

## Handling employees whose sales fall behind

Owner-selected direction: **starts casually, becoming firmer if the problem continues**. His friendly everyday manner and responsibility for results are both present; his first response is not automatically a formal warning.

The following stages and dialogue are proposed expressions of that confirmed direction, not new owner-approved disciplinary rules:

| Situation | Approach | Example voice |
| --- | --- | --- |
| First observed shortfall | A casual check-in about how things are going and what is getting in the way | "How are those accounts treating you? Anything getting stuck?" |
| Shortfall continues after an earlier conversation | More specific follow-up using actual results and the previously discussed issue | "We talked about those follow-ups. Walk me through what happened since then." |
| Repeated unresolved problem | A firm conversation about what needs to improve and a concrete follow-up point | "I need a clear plan for getting this back on track. What can you deliver, and by when?" |
| Recorded improvement | Acknowledge the progress and adjust the follow-up to the current situation | "Those follow-ups are moving again. Good work—keep me posted on the two still outstanding." |

He remembers who he spoke to, what concern was raised, any agreed next step and the later results. One poor outcome or failed roll does not invent a history of underperformance. Repeated button clicks or reloads do not advance the conversation from casual to firm. A remembered concern must be updated when results change rather than trap the employee in permanent escalation.

Use employee-specific sales records and actual work opportunities. A lack of assigned accounts, an approved absence or a known product/document blocker cannot be silently narrated as laziness. The precise sales targets, review period and any disciplinary consequences remain open design decisions. This personality choice does not automatically authorize firing, fines, pay deductions or a confrontation with Richard.

## Proposed work rhythm

These timings are authoring proposals, not new owner-approved schedule values. His confirmed responsibilities should shape the actual schedule rather than appear only in a biography.

- Early workday: review individual sales and current commitments; identify a practical blocker or priority for the team.
- Morning: follow up with staff and check the latest approved product information or pitch material.
- Midday: a real lunch/break window around the team's staggered availability.
- Afternoon: work on sales reports, pitch preparation or an arranged exchange with Richard; continue checking material sales changes.
- End of workday: leave a usable handover and record the next unresolved priority.
- Monthly: prepare and deliver the sales team's company safety briefing in an available room at a recorded time.

A pitch meeting, urgent account issue or monthly briefing can displace a normal block. Already completed steps stay complete; he does not restart a report simply because the minute tick runs again. Movement between his sales workstation, Richard's office, conference room and break room uses the existing legal doors and travel costs.
